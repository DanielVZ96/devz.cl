About a month ago my brother created a website with the help of AI. His main goal was promoting his upcoming solo-made comic (not AI-generated btw), but it has also been a huge creative outlet for him. He's been a prolific blogger, writes deeply thought out articles, cares for his site as his personal garden on the internet, has cool art, promotes local techno-adjacent artists, he has been active in several indie web communities, making art for other indie websites (not AI-generated btw), and the list goes on. In summary he has been pouring his heart out into his website and the indie web community. Here's the website if you are curious about it: https://mvndy.cl/

He's never hidden the fact that he "vibecoded" his website, and because of this, some people thought that unfounded accusations were due. I don't know what his actual reaction was, but judging from the messages he sent me, he was deeply worried. People were accusing him of faking his art with AI, that anything he made could not be trusted and thus they'd stop engaging with any of his content. I don't know what these people thought they were doing but to me they were just bullying an artist trying to make something cool. 

> Note: I think vibecoding is a good description despite the negative connotation. I myself do a lot of vibecoding even from before generative AI was a thing.

This is why I avoid online indie communities like the plague. I like reading content, writing my own, and sometimes commenting on other peoples websites. But some people can be real assholes when they think they are in the moral highground and protected by the anonymity of the internet.

# AI IS EFFECTIVE.

I'm not saying this like a tech-bro preaching that AI can re-create GTA VI in a week. I'm saying that AI is really useful for making websites or any kind of work. Of course, you have to care about whatever you are working on, otherwise you risk getting a bunch of slop from the stochastic machinery. But if you care about the work being done, it's an unprecedented work multiplier in such a way that if I didn't use it effectively for my dayjob I'd feel like I'm not being an honest worker for my employer.

> Note: Yes. I do think it's my moral obligation to do my work as best as I can, with the best tools I can use. Of course if I thought that AI use was deeply unethical by itself, I wouldn't use it, but that's not the case for me.

I used to be able to hyper-focus on a single task and blow through the planned tasks in order to then focus on more planning or side-work that was useful for the company. Now I can do that empirically 10 times more. I say 10 times more because since I've started using agents, I can do as much as 12 times more work than before. I can track it Linear, slack messages, etc. When previously a serious bug  could take at least 3 days to fix, now is fixed in a few hours alongside other 6 bugs and 2 new features.

Yes, writing more code means the possibility of more bugs. But again, if you care for your work, you will review the code (both personally and AI-assisted) and make sure you commit it in the best state possible. This has always been the case with or without AI. People have always written slop code, and now AI just augments their slop.

Even in 2022 AI was useful. I remember that a colleague was having issues with a terraform plan, which was erroring in the least useful error message possible. He wasn't finding anything on google and was having even less success in his attempts to fix it. I told him to paste the error logs into chatgpt (they had no secrets), he refused because "AI isn't reliable and it makes lots of mistakes" as if he wasn't making all those mistakes at orders of magnitude of slower speed. I copied the error logs myself, debugged the error with chatgpt, which found a github issue that explained that the bug was a specific broken combination of dependencies and terraform versions. In a few minutes I sent my coworker the solution he had been trying to find for a few days already.

# On the ethics of using AI

As I said in a note before, the only reason I wouldn't use AI is if it was unethical. This is a deep topic and could be an article of its own but in order to summarize:

1. I don't care about intellectual property. And I think AI training is fair use of other's peoples work, including artists.
2. Job losses are a pain but banning or reducing AI usage in order to avoid job losses is akin to breaking windows in order to favor the window-making industry. Also, jobs have been being replaced to automation from way before AI. In a healthy economy that should be fine, and should translate into every member of that economy being able to earn living wages. I think the real issue is not AI but private equity, but that's another whole discussion.
3. The only possible impact so far that could be deemed negative is the environmental impact. Because of this I've reviewed it myself and I've found claims to be blown way out of proportions. AI is now a huge sector of the economy, but at the same time uses orders of magnitude less resources when compared to other industries.
4. The only claims I can get behind are impact of datacenters on local communities, and I have a whole new discussion about that that I'll save for another post.

# In conclusion

Please lets just stop bullying people on the internet for using AI, specially artists using it to make cool websites for their art.
