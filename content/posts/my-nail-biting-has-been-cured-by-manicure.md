This will be a short post but I can't not share it. I've been biting my nails for as long as I can remember. It's never been a big issue besides the obvious occasional nuisance of not having nails long enough for mechanisms that require fingernails to be operated. 

> Note: Here's a list of stuff I had to operate with help of a knife or spoon, or find a workaround:
> - Nintendo Switch: Kickstand & MicroSD Slot
> - Soda & Sparkling Water Can Tabs (the workaround is to slightly raise it by pressing my thumb on the opposite end of the tab)
> - Plastic Film Seals, Stickers, Scotch tape, etc
> - Tiny Necklace Clasps
> - I always had trouble lifting thin card-like objects from a flat surface

So one day my wife was having her nails done at home and she offered if I wanted to have mine done. I usually just say no to these offerings because I thought my nails were a mess and beyond any possibility of being repaired. But having read an example of someone that stopped biting his nails by caring for them and regular manicure in Atomic Habits, I figured I might as well try myself.

After that day my nails felt like never before. It's hard to explain but for the first few days touching my soft nails felt good, and I didn't have to worry about cutting myself with those tiny leftover nail spikes from my biting sessions. Then once my nails grew, it felt as if I had grown new useful appendages. I can pinch stuff hard like never before, I can easily open the nintendo switch kickstand, or any nail-operated mechanism.

> Note: fingernails are classified as skin appendages (also called epidermal appendages), putting them in the same category as hair, sweat glands, and oil glands. Organs are made up of multiple tissues, and fingernails are dead cells that turn into keratin. The actual organ is right under your cuticule and it's called the "Nail Matrix" (what a cool name), and it constantly generates new cells that harden with keratin, pushing your nails outward.

# Novel Qualia

> From [wikipedia](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qualia): _Qualia is a term philosophers use for bits of perception or subjective, conscious experience. It is in the field called the philosophy of psychology, or philosophy of mind.
> Examples of qualia include the pain of a headache, the taste of wine, or the perceived redness of an evening sky. It is a separate bit of experience, different from thinking about propositions or stream of consciousness thought._

The weirdest thing is when I first started getting the feel of water under my nails. My wife says she doesn't feel anything under her nails in the shower, but I guess since I've virtually never had long nails the experience is so novel my brain has never experienced something like that. It's an inexplicable feeling, similar to tickling but it doesn't make me uncomfortable. And I must say I do quite enjoy it.

# The Cure

In Atomic Habits, my fellow ex-nail-biting peer explained that he started being proud of how good his nails looked, and that pushed him to notice them more and care for them. In my case it wasn't like that. I'm still not proud of my nails, but for the first few days after my manicure I was VERY observant of my nail-biting because I had payed good money for those nails! I'm not a scrooge myself, and I think I can be quite generous. But I do try to get the best run for my money (eg. not buying fast fashion clothing). So the first element of the cure was trying to get the best run for my money spent on my nails. Then after they grew, the usefulness of my new nails made me care so much for them that I just want to never ever bite them again.

I just don't want to loose being able to pinch stuff and most importantly feel water running under my nails.

I hope this helps someone to finally get to stop biting their nails.
