Whenever my wife and I are too tired to get up and go to work, or have to do something we really would love to just not do we recite to one another:

> "For the colony!"

and sometimes add:

> "And all the oppressed bugs of the world!"

and top it with a good laugh.

This is a quote from the cult classic children's movie "A Bug's Life", where Flik the protagonist, who is an Ant, embarks on a treacherous journey to find the ruthless warriors that would supposedly save his colony from the evil and exploitative grasshoppers. This is a key moment of the movie where Flik has to abandon the endangered safety of his colony to go on a quest that he'd otherwise never do. Nobody believed in him, and the journey was a farce the council of ants resorted to just get rid of Flik. But Flik doesn't flinch and literally launches himself into the unknown fueled by a sense of duty.

I like to think of this as a way to cope with the absurdity of modern life. We work our asses off. We spend a significant amount of time stuck in traffic or in public transport. All of this to arrive at home at night, too tired to do anything meaningful other than prepare food for the next day and distract ourselves with a good movie or show. Rinse and repeat.

Of course "A bugs life" not a 1-to-1 perfectly matching metaphor for our daily grinding but oh boy does it feel like it. So instead of citing the timid and semantically packed "One must imagine Sisyphus happy", my wife and I hype one another remembering the absurdity of Flik's journey.

# "What a day, Vic"

This is another one we tell one another but in the inverse situation. After a long day of work, specially on those days that everything goes wrong and you spend hours looking at a Kubernetes dashboard trying to figure out why all pods are in a CrashLoopBackoff state, and we just want to get all that load of stress off our backs.

This is from a personal anecdote where during the pandemic I spent the whole day debugging some issues we were having with our Kubernetes clusters (yes, multiple clusters) with Victor, the VP of Engineering. My wife saw the whole saga until late in the evening when we finally had fixed everything and I told Victor "What a day, Vic". It was phrased in such a way that it seemed like a neutral latinoamerican spanish movie dub. Later right before going to sleep she asked me about that and we had a good laugh.

Ever since, whenever we feel tired after a day full of challenges or just too many responsibilities handled, we recite to each other "What a day, Vic". To me it has a slight soothing comedical effect but also lets me reflect on everything we spent our time and energy on, aknowledge it, and be proud of it. We all go through rough days from time to time, and at the end of them it's good to just be happy that you were able to go through it.

# "Everyone is crazy, except us"

This one might be a bit toxic but it's something we say to the other whenever we go through an uncomfortable interaction with a third party. Of course, as you'd guess based on the other examples, it's always accompanied by a good laugh in view of the preposterousness of the phrase.

There's no way to control other people's actions, and in some cases their intentions and thought processes are impossible to decipher. In such cases I don't think it's "sane" to get personaly entangled on other's rudeness. Maybe they've just had a bad day, or are suffering a sever migraine, but to us they are just the hostile otherness we have to endure.

On the other hand, that's pretty much what someone that's departed from reality would say. And it's a way of aknowledging that maybe we are the ones in the wrong but we just don't notice it.

Either way, human interactions are complex, sometimes unsurmountable. Everyday you interact with hundreds or thousands of people, and if you let every negative interaction get the best of you, then you'll be another grumpy passerby in the absurdity of a modern city.

# I'm sure there are more but I don't remember them all right now

With my wife, we have loads of these kind of phrases to soothe each other. I have the hunch that everyone has a variation of these, so I'm curious which ones are yours? Please reach out elsewhere and share them with me, or do your own post and webmention this one.
